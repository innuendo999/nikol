How to Pack a Makeup Bag for Travel: A Makeup Artist's Guide

This time I unpack my travel makeup bag and do a full look with it, right in a tiny hotel room in Odesa after a night on the train. The products are chosen sparingly and with purpose, so they work for both a day and an evening look. Here is the step-by-step guide, including why I apply foundation with a dry sponge.

The routine in short

  1. Toner, vitamin C serum and SPF
  2. Glow filter and a first pass of setting spray
  3. Concealer under the eyes only, don't cover everything
  4. Light foundation with a dry sponge
  5. Cream bronzer warmed on the palm
  6. Cheeks: highlighter and blush 1 : 1
  7. Eyes, lashes and brows
  8. Set again, lips and a dry powder correction

Who this guide is for

For anyone who wants one small bag for a trip and still a day and an evening look. No extras: half of these products do two jobs at once.

What to pack

Everything in my bag. Tick off what you have packed:

Five brushes are enough

Five brushes are all I need for a trip: four angled and one flat. The angled ones are for contour, blush, concealer and dry textures; the flat one is for eyes. I really don't need more.

Step-by-step guide

Pick a version: Minimal is the look without eyeshadow and lips, Day is exactly what I do in the video, and Evening adds two extra steps. Tap a step to see on the face where the product goes.

Photo from my portfolio: a natural day look

  1. Medi-Peel Hyal Kombucha Tea-Tox Toner

    My face is already washed, but I use toner for extra hydration, a final cleanse and gentle exfoliation: the cotton pad rubbing over the skin is enough. I go over the whole face and always down the neck. I keep the pad, because later I'll wipe my hand on it between products.

  2. Image Skincare Vital C

    My vitamin C for many years. It hydrates, doesn't weigh the skin down, smells lovely and comes in packaging that keeps it from oxidising. Pricier, but worth it.

  3. Round Lab Birch Juice SPF 50+

    A classic I never go without in the sun. Ideally let the skincare sink in for a moment. When you're travelling, there's rarely time.

  4. Dior Forever Glow Star Filter, shade 2

    In summer I go one shade deeper to match my tan. One pump is enough for a glowing effect, and the rest goes under the eyes. It's hydrating: in summer my T-zone can get oily and wear is shorter than in the colder months. That doesn't bother me.

  5. Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Setting Spray

    I set what's on my skin so the base sits better and lasts longer once I'm out in the heat. Let it dry.

  6. Note Cosmetique BB Concealer 03

    My new love: it covers my under-eye circles beautifully. It's quite dark on me, so I use it under the eyes only. You need very little; press it in with a sponge.

    Pro tip

    In day makeup, especially in summer, don't cover the circles completely. It looks unnatural.

  7. Estée Lauder Double Wear Sheer, 2N1

    The lighter version of Double Wear: less long-wearing, slightly hydrating, and it suits probably every skin type except very dry. Light coverage that stays. It has less pigment: it evens out the tone, but don't expect miracle coverage. Today I don't want perfect skin, just to look fresh, not like I spent two nights on a train.

  8. Eveline Choco Glamour 02

    A cooler shade. I pick it up on my hand and warm it: I used to use the back of my hand, but it's dry from work, so now I use the inside of my palm. It's warmer too, so the product melts faster. It blends easily, mixes with other products, and if you take too much it wipes off easily. Then I wipe my hand on the saved cotton pad.

  9. Dior Forever Glow Maximizer Nude · YSL Make Me Blush 44

    My new combo. Glow Maximizer is half blush, half highlighter, and it sets. I mix it with the YSL blush roughly one to one, so it sets the blush too and the cheeks need nothing else: colour and glow in one. For a blush with a soft transition, see my transition blush guide.

  10. Cream eyeshadow stick · Hourglass Ambient Lighting Edit · Davydenko flat brush no. 7

    I blend a warm golden-rose cream shadow with a finger: a thin layer, it doesn't crease, and it's fast. With the dark powder from the palette and a flat brush I set the fixed part of the lid, because any cream product fades during the day. A deeper shade of bronzer goes on the outer corner, swept towards the temple, and a little under the lower lash line.

    If your hand slips, go over it with a brush that still has some concealer on it and pat with the sponge.

  11. Eveline Variété Lashes Show Brown

    A dark brown mascara I stock up on in Ukraine, because in the Czech Republic it costs several times more. Mine was drying out, so I added a drop of silicone makeup thinner and it works again, it just transfers a little.

    Note

    Mascara has a short period after opening. Thinning it is an emergency fix for a trip, not a replacement for a new tube.

  12. Patricia Ledo Absolute Eyebrow 01 · Physicians Formula liner · Benefit 24-Hour Brow Setter

    I fill the shape with the pencil. I bought the Physicians Formula liner to draw freckles after seeing the hack somewhere. I didn't like the freckles, but now I use the liner for a few extra hair strokes, no statement brows. Benefit gel: brush up first, then sideways. My brows are medium-coarse, so I don't glue them; I just bring out their natural shape.

  13. Setting spray

    One more pass of setting spray. The Charlotte Tilbury one is quite wet and the nozzle spits rather than mists. I've just bought the Fenty Beauty spray, and its fine mist is a different story.

  14. Davydenko Beauty lip pencil 05

    With this kind of makeup I don't want bold lips. For many people this pencil will look dark, but my natural lip pigment is quite strong and dark, so for me it's a nude.

  15. Powder from the Hourglass palette · brush for dry textures

    I pick up powder from the palette, mix in a little bronzer and press it exactly where I used the cream bronzer. It sets the cream so it lasts longer.

  16. Sephora brightening concealer

    A very light, hydrating concealer I use in the evening to brighten selected areas. I don't need it during the day.

  17. Pupa lip pencil 004

    A well-known cool pink pencil. It lives in my bag in case I want different lips in the evening.

Dry or damp sponge?

People may throw stones at me for this, but I apply foundation with a dry sponge. I like how densely it covers. With a damp sponge the coverage is sheerer. My sponge is soft enough that working dry-on-dry is fine. For a heavier foundation, though, I do dampen it.

Which brown mascara to choose

Eveline Variété is dark brown, not light brown. On me, with my high-contrast colouring, it looks great. A lighter, more reddish brown is the Essence brown mascara, which doesn't look good on me.

Your colouring

High contrast

Dark brown

Eveline Variété Lashes Show Brown: dark enough for high-contrast colouring, yet softer than black.

Fair hair

Reddish brown

Essence brown mascara: lighter and more reddish. It looks lovely on blondes.

Red hair

Reddish brown

Essence brown mascara: the warm reddish tone suits red hair.

Why one palette is enough

The Hourglass Ambient Lighting Edit palette was a great buy. You don't need them all, but I recommend one limited edition in colours you love: the textures are baked, soft and semi-sheer, they can be applied thinly or built up, and they all mix together. I rarely wear bright blush, but I use it as a colour mixer for the cheeks or eye makeup. The golden highlighter mixed with blush looks beautiful on tanned skin.

What goes in a travel makeup bag

The basics

  • Toner in a small bottle
  • Vitamin C serum and SPF
  • Light foundation and concealer
  • Cream bronzer
  • Dark brown mascara
  • Brow gel
  • Setting spray

Two jobs in one

  • Palette: powder, blush, eyeshadow, highlighter
  • Glow Maximizer: blush and highlighter
  • Bronzer: contour and eyeshadow
  • Brow liner

Evening only

  • Sephora brightening concealer
  • Pupa 004 cool pink lip pencil

Three things to take away

  1. 1

    Fewer products, more jobs.

    Bronzer as contour and eyeshadow, one palette instead of five compacts. The bag stays small and still covers day and evening.

  2. 2

    Set in layers.

    Spray after the base, powder over cream eyeshadow and bronzer. In the heat, cream products last only as long as you set them.

  3. 3

    Don't cover everything by day.

    Concealer only where needed, a light foundation, and freshness instead of perfect skin.

Frequently asked questions

What should I pack in a travel makeup bag?

+

Skincare (toner, serum, SPF), a light foundation, concealer, cream bronzer, blush, a face palette, mascara, brow pencil and gel, a lip pencil, setting spray, a few brushes and a sponge. Choose products that do more than one job.

How many brushes do I need for travel?

+

Five are enough for me: four angled ones for contour, blush, concealer and dry textures, and one flat brush for eyes. Cream eyeshadow can be blended with a finger.

Should I apply foundation with a dry or damp sponge?

+

A dry sponge gives denser coverage, a damp one a sheerer finish. I apply a light foundation with a dry sponge because mine is soft enough. A heavier foundation goes on with a damp sponge.

How do I make makeup last in the heat?

+

Set it in layers: spray after the base, powder over cream eyeshadow and over the cream bronzer, and spray again at the end. Hydrating products wear a little shorter in summer.

Why warm cream bronzer on the palm?

+

The product melts faster on the palm and blends more easily. The inside of the palm is warmer than the back of the hand and doesn't dry out as much.

Which brown mascara should I choose?

+

Dark brown for high-contrast colouring, such as Eveline Variété Lashes Show Brown. Reddish brown for fair or red hair, such as the Essence brown mascara.
