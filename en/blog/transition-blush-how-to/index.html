Transition Blush: How to Get the Blurred Blush Without a New Kit

Patrick Ta launched his "transition blurring blushes" and my TikTok is flooded with them. Is he a genius, or is he selling a technique that has been around for twenty years? Do we all have to rush out and buy something? And does it really suit every face? I'll show you how transition blush works, where it came from and how to do it with what you already own.

The routine in short

  1. Mix the light and, separately, the deep blush shades
  2. Light shade by the inner corner, blended down
  3. Deeper shade on the prominent cheekbone, not the apple
  4. Blend the edges with the light shade
  5. Concealer mixed with blush on dark circles
  6. Set with powder
  7. For evening, top up with a bright blush and a big brush

Who it is for

For anyone who wants a blurred blush without a hard edge and doesn't want to buy a new collection for it. A concealer, one or two blushes and a big brush are enough. I use the technique on clients too.

Who came up with it

Transition blush isn't new. A short history, so you know who to credit:

  1. The origin

    Kevyn Aucoin

    The legendary makeup artist introduced the technique as "transition blush" and wrote about it in his book. His looks are supermodels of the 80s, 90s and 2000s, no bright colours. His brand makes dry products for it with a texture I really like.

  2. Last year

    Paint By Ester

    The makeup artist adapted the technique and made it more vibrant. She openly showed how she does it in a public masterclass. The main trendsetter of the past year.

  3. A few weeks ago

    Patrick Ta

    The American celebrity makeup artist launched a whole line "designed for transition blush": a palette of dry textures, a blush-corrector stick, plus a palette of dry and cream blushes. He mentions neither of his predecessors, and the collection got a lot of hate.

How transition blush works

The principle is simple: between the blush and the skin you create an intermediate layer of product that has a subtle blush tint and matches your skin perfectly. The colour then doesn't jump from skin straight into a saturated patch, it moves gradually. All you need is concealer and blush, in one or several shades, and dry products work fine.

As a professional, Patrick Ta knows how to do this without those products. And you don't have to be a makeup artist to do it with what you have at home. Many of you already do it; you just didn't know it had a name.

Mix your own transition

The whole secret: mix concealer with blush in several proportions. The lightest, a bit deeper, deeper still, until you reach the colour of the blush itself. You can have as many transitions as you like, and you don't need to buy a million extra products. Try it:

Step-by-step guide

Pick a version: Kevyn Aucoin is the soft day version, Paint By Ester adds a big brush, concealer mixed with blush and powder, and the evening top-up adds extra colour. Tap a step to see on the photo where the product goes.

Photo from my portfolio

  1. Two blushes · the lightest concealer

    First I mix the lightest blushes into a soft transition shade, a lovely lilac on me. Then, separately, I mix the darkest ones: those are the main blushes. You can easily do all of this with a single blush. So far I only have foundation and a little bronzer on, no concealer.

  2. Light lilac-pink mix

    The lighter lilac-pink shade goes to the very inner corner of the eye, blended slightly downward. I don't take it under the crease; there's no need. I try to apply it already blended, so I don't have to fix anything afterwards.

  3. Deeper blush · a brush

    I pick up the deeper shade and apply it to the prominent part of the cheekbone, not the apple. I go right up under the eye while still avoiding the under-eye area, and a little out to the side. The higher you take it, the wider the face looks; from the side it gives a slight lifting effect.

  4. What is left of the deeper blush

    With the leftovers you can sweep over the nose and blend the rest out to the sides. Optional.

  5. Light mix

    I go back to the lighter shade and run it over the edges so the deeper and lighter shades melt together. With Kevyn Aucoin's technique you could stop here; he would use a less bright concealer under the eyes.

    Pro tip

    Always blend the edges so the blush doesn't look like a stain. If it's too much, go back to the lighter shade and blend it out.

  6. Large fluffy brush · blush

    Paint By Ester uses a very large, fluffy brush and picks up the product carefully. She first spreads the blush as widely as possible, so the first layer is less intense and already blended. When I tried it I thought: genius. A large brush blends more softly. With a very dense brush it won't look as nice.

  7. Concealer mixed with blush

    I cover the dark circles with concealer mixed with blush. You don't have to go all the way down into the under-eye triangle. My face is more oval and my cheeks aren't very full, so it suits me better without extra brightness there, only where there's shadow.

  8. Leftover concealer

    In the same way I clean up the liner with the leftover product, carefully, so nothing smudges. It elongates it visually even more. If you feel you went overboard, run a little light concealer over the edges.

  9. Fenty Beauty powder, light lilac

    Paint By Ester sets everything with powder. Mine is a light lilac from Fenty, because that's the shade I'm using today. You can bake if you like, but with today's makeup I don't need to. I love running it down the bridge of the nose to highlight it a little.

  10. Bright blush · large brush

    If that's not enough, pick up a bright blush with a large brush and emphasise the features you want to draw attention to: like a mask, or, as I do, on the cheekbones to elongate the face. There's no harsh edge, because underneath you've already built a base from skin tone through the lightest pink to a very deep pink.

  11. Darkest blush

    I put the darkest blush on the lips and a little on the eyes, so everything comes together.

  12. A clean sponge · your foundation

    If the evening Instagram or TikTok version isn't for you, pick up the same foundation you're wearing with a clean sponge and press it over gently. Not thick, not fully opaque. The result is instantly more wearable.

Why a big fluffy brush

It's really just a slightly different order of steps, but the brush makes the difference. A big fluffy brush lays the product over a large area, lighter and already blended. A dense brush leaves a saturated patch with an edge you then have to blend out. Move the slider:

Does transition blush suit every face?

The technique suits everyone. Where you place the blush, though, depends on your face shape. Here's how it works on me and on a round face:

Face shape

Round face

Brighten the triangle

Blush placed high and brightness in the triangle under the eye lift the face and make it look more cheerful. The triangle works: the face narrows where the deeper shade sits, and the light part adds brightness and definition.

Oval face

Brighten only the shadow

Don't go into the whole triangle. Add brightness only where there's darkness. Extra brightness on an oval face without full cheeks can look puffy.

If you'd like to know where blush goes for each face shape, tell me in the comments under the video. And if you want to try it on your own face, that's exactly what we cover in a one-on-one makeup course.

Do you need Patrick Ta's collection?

All you need

  • The lightest concealer
  • One or two blushes
  • A big fluffy brush
  • Setting powder

Only if you want to

  • Patrick Ta's collection (good products, about $120)
  • Kevyn Aucoin dry products

Watch out for

  • One saturated patch with no transition
  • A dense brush for the first layer
  • Unblended edges

Three things to take away

  1. 1

    You can mix the transition yourself.

    Concealer and blush in a few proportions replace a whole collection. I just saved you about 120 dollars.

  2. 2

    A big brush does half the work.

    A fluffy brush applies lighter and already blended, so you never get a stain.

  3. 3

    The technique suits everyone; placement doesn't.

    A round face benefits from brightness in the triangle, an oval one rather only where there is shadow.

Frequently asked questions

What is transition blush?

+

A technique where you build an intermediate layer between skin and blush: concealer mixed with a little blush. The colour then moves from skin to blush gradually, without a hard edge.

Who invented transition blush?

+

The makeup artist Kevyn Aucoin introduced it as "transition blush" and wrote about it in his book. Paint By Ester popularised a more vibrant version last year. Patrick Ta recently launched a product collection for it.

Do I need special products for transition blush?

+

No. The lightest concealer, one or two blushes and a big fluffy brush are enough. Mix the concealer with the blush in a few proportions and you have your transitions.

Where do I apply blush for transition blush?

+

The deeper shade goes on the prominent part of the cheekbone, not the apple, right up under the eye and a little out to the side. Then blend the edges with the lighter shade.

Does transition blush suit everyone?

+

The technique does, but blush placement depends on face shape. A round face benefits from brightness in the under-eye triangle; an oval face without full cheeks rather only where there is shadow.

What if there's too much blush?

+

Pick up the same foundation you are wearing with a clean sponge and press it over gently, not to full coverage. Or blend the edges out with the lighter shade.
