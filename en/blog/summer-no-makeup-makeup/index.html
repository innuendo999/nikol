Summer No-Makeup Makeup: A Makeup Artist's Routine for Hot Days

By Nikol Pavlova – Professional Makeup Artist, Prague

No-makeup makeup is my most worn look every summer. Skin should look fresh and light, it must survive the heat, and above all I want to be able to wash it off, reapply SPF and redo it in ten minutes. In this tutorial I go through the whole routine step by step and tell you what I honestly think of every product as a makeup artist.

The routine in short

  1. Toner and SPF 50, including neck and décolleté
  2. Match foundation to the hollow above your breastbone, not your neck
  3. A light fluid only where you need it
  4. A blurring silicone primer only on the T-zone and shadows
  5. A cream bronzer for warmth
  6. A cream palette with fingers for circles and redness
  7. Brows, mascara, powder only where it shines
  8. Setting spray and a touch of highlighter

Who it is for and how I tested it

This routine isn't for you if you want full coverage or a statement evening look. It's for beach days, holidays and hot city days when you want to look rested, not "done". In the video I wear a lighter version; below I also describe the steps you can add.

My skin is combination and has become oilier with age and heat, especially when I don't drink enough water. I have freckles with a warm khaki undertone, redness around the nose and a cool purple shadow under one eye. I had been wearing the base for a few hours before filming and it still looked fresh.

Step 1: skin prep and SPF

I usually skip foaming cleansers; my skin doesn't need them. I start with the Medicube Exosome Cica Toner: thin, light and without that "just washed" tight feeling. It suits oily skin too.

Next comes Paula's Choice 5% Vitamin C Sheer Moisturizer SPF 50. For me it's three in one: it hydrates, protects and brightens, so in summer I skip serum and moisturiser. The texture is liquid and not sticky, which helps anyone who hates the feel of sunscreen on the face. I apply it under the eyes, on the neck and on the décolleté too.

Note

Vitamin C can irritate sensitive skin. If your skin is reactive, patch-test the SPF first.

Step 2: foundation shades for freckled skin

Freckled skin with a cool, pinkish undertone easily looks grey in a light foundation. You need to warm the complexion up, but not so much that the face turns orange while the body stays white. What works for me is Chanel Les Beiges in B30: the warm tone keeps freckles from going grey.

Pro tip

Don't match your foundation to your neck. It often doesn't tan and is usually lighter. Match it to the hollow above your breastbone, between the neck and the chest.

People with freckles tend to have them on the back, arms and shoulders, but rarely on the neck. That's why the neck is misleading when you choose a shade.

Step 3: light fluids and CC creams

I compare two Chanel fluids. Les Beiges Water-Fresh Tint only slightly evens out the skin and doesn't cover redness. For me it's almost pointless; I'm only using it up so it doesn't expire. Les Beiges Water-Fresh Complexion Touch covers noticeably more, lasts well and I would buy it again even though it's expensive. I reach for it on days when I have no time and want to cover my red nose quickly.

The lighter Tint is usually better for oily skin. Both last about the same and can wear off a little during the day, which doesn't matter for a no-makeup look.

I also show two CC creams. CU Skin Vitamin U CC Cream SPF 40 leans orange and suits a lot of people, but test the shade in store. SPF 40 alone isn't enough for a holiday, so you need extra SPF underneath. Erborian CC Red Correct works better for fair skin, skin that is only starting to tan, or days at home.

Step 4: blur instead of shine

My new phase: instead of one base product I layer several, and above all I use blurring silicone primers only where I don't want shine. Glow looks beautiful on perfectly smooth skin without visible pores or shadows. My skin is starting to show the first changes, like a new crease, so I no longer make the whole face glow.

I apply them to the T-zone, a little on the forehead, along the sides of the nose and into the hollows where shadows form. To find the right spots, turn your face towards the light and look at where it reflects.

  • Hourglass Veil Mineral Primer is the one I use mostly on clients.
  • Dior Forever Glow Star Filter and Diorskin Forever are a little lighter but oilier, and work less well for me professionally.
  • For myself I use the cheaper tinted e.l.f. Halo Glow Liquid Filter; it's greasier, so not for clients.

Shake the product well so the pigment mixes. One pump is enough. I apply it with a finger and use a brush for precise areas, and I don't forget the ears, hairline, collarbones and chest. The goal isn't a flat face but a satin finish: a balance of glow and blur.

Step 5: warmth

As a cream bronzer I use a mini Charlotte Tilbury Hollywood Flawless Filter in shade 6.5. It lasts, reflects light and looks natural on the skin. It does have a distinctly orange undertone that won't suit everyone. On freckled skin in summer, I love it.

You can stop here: add lip gloss, highlighter and brows, and the look is done.

Step 6: correcting without concealer

For under-eye circles, redness and soft shading I use the Make Up For Ever HD Skin Face Essentials cream palette, which I'd wanted for a long time and finally bought at a masterclass. It has shades for circles, redness and bronzing, and the blushes are amazing.

It isn't for weddings or clients who need long wear and full coverage, though. It's delicate, less pigmented and wears off easily. Great for photo shoots, not for a full day. It's an expensive product, so it's worth knowing its limits.

Under-eye technique: press the second-lightest shade with one finger into the darkest part of the circle, and blend only the edges with another finger. Leave the centre alone; that's where you want the most pigment. I rarely put concealer under my eyes because the skin creases with expression, and for no-makeup makeup less is more.

Tap the redness with a finger, add a soft contour under the cheekbones and a little light on the forehead for dimension. Any light, non-greasy silicone-based foundation can replace the palette; I used Estée Lauder Double Wear Sheer only on redness, not under the eyes. For details I use the NYX Bare With Me serum concealer in Vanilla, which I tested in my full NYX review. I don't fully cover the bruise under my eye every day; with full coverage I don't feel as lively.

Step 7: brows

I fill the gaps with the dry, powdery ZOLA Powder Brow Pencil in Blond, which has great pigment, and set them with NYX The Brow Glue. That's all.

Step 8: cheeks and lips with fingers

For a sun-kissed effect I add just a touch of bronzer; I rarely wear blush and bronzer together. I apply the cream with one finger and blend with a clean one. There's no foundation on the skin, so everything melts in easily. I put the same product on my lips with my fingers.

If something looks too strong, I go over it with what's left of the concealer.

Step 9: lashes

My lashes are long and straight, so I curl them. I curl one eye, do my makeup, then curl the other, because the curl drops quickly. I use Lancôme Lash Idôle in brown, which unlike many mascaras never flakes. I apply it to the lower lashes too. Some days I skip lashes altogether.

Step 10: powder only where you need it, and lips

The e.l.f. High Definition Powder in Sheer is incredible and you need very little. I pick it up with a brush, tap almost all of it off and press it only where I don't want shine: under the eyes, on the chin, around the nose and on the forehead. Above all I press it over concealer so it doesn't crease. The skin looks blurred and set, but not powdery or dry. If you want a soft powder glow, the second thing I bought at the masterclass is the Hourglass Ambient Lighting Palette Volume 3.

On the lips I used lip pencil 01 and on top Chanel Rouge Coco Flash in 54 Boy, which I discovered recently and fell in love with. I apply it slightly messy on purpose so it looks natural. As an alternative I like the unico Lip Gloss With Menthol in Crush.

Step 11: setting sprays, tested

I own several setting sprays, so I compared them:

Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Finish
my favourite for myself and for work; sets and adds a nice glow
Fenty Beauty You Mist
lovely glow and scent, but cakes if you drench it over baked powder
Caudalie Beauty Elixir
doesn't set; hydrates and smells like summer; contains alcohol
d'Alba White Truffle First Spray Serum
not a setting spray; a hydrating spray serum for dry skin
e.l.f. and Sephora minis
don't set anything at all
Heavy-duty setting sprays
hairspray for the face; pointless for light makeup

With Charlotte Tilbury, spray once to the side first so the big droplets don't land on your face. I've used Caudalie since 2021, when I moved to the Czech Republic; this is probably my third or fourth bottle. If alcohol in skincare doesn't agree with you, check the ingredients. In the video I also show L'Oréal Paris Infaillible 3-Second Setting Mist and Givenchy Prisme Libre Prep & Set Glow Mist.

Step 12: glow and SPF during the day

I tap a little Charlotte Tilbury Beauty Light Wand with a soft rosy tone onto my cheekbones with a finger. Shimmer sticks on the outer face work, but not on skin with texture, enlarged pores or scarring. During the day I don't overdo accents; in the evening, when the light changes, I add more bronzer and blush. You can see the bolder version on my evening makeup page.

SPF during the day: I don't layer cream SPF over makeup; on dusty skin under makeup I don't think it's a good idea. That's exactly why the routine is so light: at the beach I wash my face, reapply SPF and redo it in a few minutes.

The verdict: what is worth buying

Worth buying

  • Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Finish
  • Lancôme Lash Idôle, brown
  • NYX Bare With Me
  • e.l.f. High Definition Powder
  • Chanel Rouge Coco Flash 54
  • Chanel Water-Fresh Complexion Touch
  • Caudalie Beauty Elixir (hydration)

Depends on your skin

  • Hollywood Flawless Filter (orange undertone)
  • Make Up For Ever HD Skin Face Essentials (short wear)
  • Fenty Beauty You Mist (use lightly)
  • Paula's Choice SPF 50 with vitamin C (sensitive skin)

Skip

  • Chanel Water-Fresh Tint (on my skin)
  • e.l.f. and Sephora mini sprays
  • Heavy-duty sprays on light makeup

Three things to take away

  1. 1

    Match your chest, not your neck.

    On freckled skin the warm undertone matters most. A grey face gives away the wrong shade more than any coverage does.

  2. 2

    Blur only where you need it.

    Leave the rest of the skin naturally satin. A fully matte face never looks fresh in summer.

  3. 3

    Less concealer under the eyes.

    Press pigment into the darkest spot and blend only the edges. That way the makeup doesn't crease.

Frequently asked questions

What is no-makeup makeup?

+

A light look that evens out the skin, corrects only what shows and enhances your features so the makeup isn't noticeable. Instead of full coverage it relies on light fluids, cream products and powder only in selected areas.

How do I choose a foundation shade if I have freckles?

+

Match it to the hollow above your breastbone, not to your neck, which is usually lighter. Freckled skin with a cool undertone needs a slightly warmer shade, otherwise it looks grey, but not so warm that the face turns orange.

How do I control shine in summer without looking flat?

+

Put a blurring silicone primer only on the T-zone, around the nose and in the hollows where shadows form, and press powder into the same areas. Leave the rest of the skin satin.

Which setting spray is best for summer?

+

For setting, Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Finish worked best for me; for hydration, Caudalie Beauty Elixir. In my test the e.l.f. and Sephora mini sprays didn't set at all.

Can I reapply SPF over makeup during the day?

+

I don't layer cream SPF over makeup; on dusty skin I don't think it's a good idea. That's why this routine is so light: you can wash it off, reapply SPF and redo it in a few minutes.
